## What

A standards repository that a project can adopt without taking on any other standards
repository — no runtime dependency, no build dependency, no submodule, no path reference. The
proven policy-as-code machinery was vendored by copy from EngineeringStandards and adapted where
the innovation domain genuinely differs.

## Why

Independence was the constraint that shaped everything else. A shared engine would have made
every pack's release a coordination problem, and would have let one domain's needs silently
redefine another's.

## Notes

The cost is real and was accepted with its eyes open: the engine now exists in more than one
place, and a fix to one copy does not reach the others. That trade is argued in
[ADR 0001](../../adr/) and in proposal 0001, which is the repository's own new-project
justification.

**Reopened 2026-08-12**, having been COMPLETE and closed on 2026-08-09. Proposal 0004 reached a
`build` decision, which produced [EP-02](EP-02.md) and the first authorized implementation work under
this initiative. The closed date was removed rather than kept beside an open subtree — a completion
record that no longer describes the tree is worse than no record. The drop from 100% is the
denominator changing because new work crossed the ownership boundary, which is the behaviour
[SCOPE.md](../SCOPE.md) describes as healthy.

The architecture has since been tested by events rather than by argument. A concurrent session
added a StandardsEnforcer adapter declaration here without the pack needing to know anything
about the enforcer, and the first external adoption (HouseDoc) consumed the pack through a
pinned tag. Neither required a dependency in either direction.
