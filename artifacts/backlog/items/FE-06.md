## What

Eleven fixture repositories covering the compliant cases, each defect class, and the
mentions-only negative case; plus suites for the engine semantics, the policy, the audit
invariants, the proposal detectors, and the integrity invariant's own protection.

## Why

A check nobody has seen fail is not known to work. Every defect fixture was confirmed to fire its
exact rules **before** the passing fixtures were trusted, and the suite asserts both directions.

## Notes

The mutation discipline earned its cost immediately. An early diagram check paired a `.mmd` to a
host document by comparing first lines and only then compared the rest — so editing the first line
matched no host and the drift reported clean. A check whose failure path can be stepped around by
the very edit it guards against is worse than no check, because it reports green. Only the
mutation test found it.

The `compliant-rejection` and `mentions-only` fixtures are the two that most often look redundant
and are not: one proves a rejection passes, the other proves the detectors cannot be triggered by
prose outside the proposals directory.

A later lesson belongs here too: a green aggregate test count is not automatically assurance you
earned. When a concurrent session's commit added four tests, "131 passing" silently included work
this repository had not reviewed.
