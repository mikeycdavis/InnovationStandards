## What

Twenty-nine rules with identity, level, severity, validation type and assurance; seven of them
invariant-class. The schema and this repository's own dogfooded policy were authored together
rather than in sequence.

## Why

Authoring the schema against a real policy is what proves the abstractions hold. Writing the real
policy is specifically what proved *not-applicable* and *exception* must remain separate
mechanisms — one says a rule has no subject here, the other says it has a subject and is being
knowingly violated for a bounded time. A schema written without a policy in front of it would
plausibly have merged them.

## Notes

The architectural rule the catalog holds, and which the whole compliance system rests on: the
catalog defines rule identity and metadata, the policy defines project applicability, the
evaluator produces evidence, and **none of the three may redefine the others**. `assertBindings`
enforces the last of those mechanically — a detector reporting an unknown rule id throws.

Every `partial` or `none` assurance rule carries a `$assuranceNote` saying what the check
establishes (presence) versus what it cannot (adequacy, truthfulness). Seven rules are
`manual-review` with assurance `none`, and that number is published rather than hidden.
