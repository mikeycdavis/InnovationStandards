## What

The investigation the domain required, written before any code existed. For each candidate
framework concept — requirement, prohibition, recommendation, applicability, evidence,
verification, exceptions, severity, invariants, revisit conditions, not-applicable,
not-evaluated, compliant, non-compliant — a verdict of adopt, adapt or reject with reasoning.
Plus the CLI design and five ADRs.

## Why

The concepts inherited from an engineering framework do not all transfer. Deciding which ones did,
on paper and with reasons, is what kept the pack from becoming a rename of its source.

## Notes

Two rejections are worth remembering because they were tempting: no `plan` subcommand, because
`init --dry-run` already *is* the mutation plan and a second planning concept would drift from it;
and no numeric compliance threshold, because status must never be computed from a score.

The most consequential design call was making the invariant class **derived** — `level: forbidden`
plus `nonExemptible: true` — rather than a new catalog field. The engine already half-enforced it,
and a declared field would have been a second thing to keep in agreement with the first.
